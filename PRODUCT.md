# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Software developers who coordinate several local AI coding agents across Jira tickets,
repositories, branches, worktrees, tests, reviews, and pull requests. They work for extended
periods on a Mac, need to understand what every agent is doing at a glance, and must be able to
take over through a real terminal without losing context.

## Product Purpose

Provide one trustworthy control surface for local Codex and Claude workflows. A developer starts
from a Jira ticket, sees the branches and assigned agents, follows implementation and independent
review, inspects terminals and artifacts, and retains explicit control over commit, push, and draft
pull request actions. Success means less terminal hunting, reliable handoffs between agents, clear
ownership per branch, and no ambiguity about which actions still require a human.

## Brand Personality

Calm, technical, direct. The interface should feel focused and dependable during deep work. It
earns confidence through precise state, familiar macOS behavior, concise language, and visible
safety boundaries. Linear and Raycast are quality references for density, speed, keyboard fluency,
and disciplined visual hierarchy.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a neon AI control room, a chat-first assistant, a wall of interchangeable cards,
or a generic SaaS dashboard. Avoid decorative gradients, glassmorphism, animated agent avatars,
oversized metrics, vague status language, and novelty interactions that obscure standard macOS
affordances.

## Design Principles

- Make ownership obvious: every ticket, branch, agent role, and pending human action has one clear place.
- Show operational truth: state, test results, review cycle, diff identity, and blockers must be explicit.
- Keep the terminal first-class: automation adds structure without hiding the underlying tools.
- Optimize for flow: dense information, fast keyboard navigation, and progressive detail beat decoration.
- Make safety visible: read-only roles and human approval boundaries must be understandable before action.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA where applicable to a native macOS interface. Support full keyboard operation,
VoiceOver labels and reading order, reduced motion, sufficient contrast, scalable text, and status
communication that never depends on color alone.
